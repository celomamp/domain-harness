# PRD Quality Review — Domain Harness

## Overall verdict

The thesis is real and the PRD is willing to say what it will not do. Vision, journeys, the first cut, and the confirmed commercial line (free puts Standards in the agent; the first paid tier is proof and control) all point at the same bet: a cross-tool Project tree with Locked rules, and a record that joins Guidance to the Gate. It is not ready to break into stories. The gate that the pilot sells cannot be accepted as done until a Standard has a conflict key, a code-stack entry can express "required," and an Accepted deviation survives a new revision; the addendum still calls that confirmed commercial line an assumption; and §13.1 commits the team to building a registry the PRD also says not to build until an adopt-or-contribute decision is made.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

A reader of `prd.md` can act. The bet is stated as a bet ("The bet is two things agent vendors do not assemble across tools"), the paid wall is named as a wall ("The paid wall is the Project tree, the Locked rule, a named Accepted deviation, and the history that joins Guidance to a later Deviation"), and the first cut is a Team-shaped pilot with Community allowed to slip. Open Questions are actually open: Question 7 is marked resolved instead of being left as a rhetorical prompt, and the `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit on real tensions (maintenance as a service, Decision-response disagreement, the Compilation field list, change-acceptance that may need to move forward). Rejected mechanisms in the addendum record what was given up.

Two commercial instructions still fight each other, one inside the PRD and one against the addendum. Until those are aligned, a pricing, security, or board reader can reopen decisions the 2026-10-09 confirmation closed, or ship the Decision agent on a tier that does not carry the retention term the FRs require.

### Findings

- **high** Addendum reopens the confirmed commercial line (§6, §16; addendum "Entitlement recommendation," "Rejected mechanisms," "Commercial recommendations," "Deployment boundary") — §6 opens with "Confirmed 2026-10-09" and §16 says the commercial line now stands as requirements, including "v1 does not charge per seat" and "The charging unit is decided" (Open Question 9). The addendum still says seat-based pricing is "Still an assumption in the PRD," that the entitlement table is "Not confirmed. The PRD §6 records the assumption," and that "Charge per Active governed Application" is one of the three board items in Open Question 9. The deployment note also says raw prompt text "is excluded by the current assumption," while FR-28 states that exclusion as a consequence. *Fix:* Update the addendum so tier contents and the charging unit are recorded as decided, leave only price points and the two remaining board items (Brazil-first, NewCo versus Levva) unconfirmed, and restate prompt exclusion as the FR-28 requirement.

- **high** Zero retention is required on Business and listed on Enterprise (§6, FR-12, FR-27) — Business includes the "Decision agent." Enterprise includes "zero retention." FR-12's feature NFR says that when the Decision agent is enabled, "Domain Harness and the model provider retain no request or response content," and FR-27 says enabling it "requires the zero-retention term." A Business contract that follows the tier list can sell Decision response without the term the FRs treat as a precondition; an Enterprise contract can look like the only place zero retention exists. *Fix:* Split the two scopes in §6. State the Decision-agent term as part of Business, and state what Enterprise adds beyond it (dedicated environment, company-wide retention, or both). Use those phrases in FR-27.

## Substance over theater — strong

The differentiation is the residue of a search, not a template section. The vision names the incumbent pieces ("Organization instructions, team rules, design-system servers, pull-request review, scorecards, and CI scanners already exist inside single products") and claims only the cross-tool tree and the join. The addendum's "What not to claim" lists Packmind, Tessl, Cortex, Port, and the design-system servers as already shipping. Three jobs (Buyer, Builder, Standard owner) each force a requirement; Vini, Ana, and Helena carry those jobs through the journeys. NFR-1 through NFR-5 are product checks (same Deviations for the same revision, no silent pass, no mixed Standard versions), and unset latency is left as Open Question 8 rather than filled with "responsive" or "scalable." Non-users exclude a scanner buyer and a design-system author, which keeps those products from leaking in as features.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

Features, packaging, and the first cut follow one arc. Locked rules and Accepted deviations are in the pilot because a Gate without an acceptance path "recreates the bottleneck the product exists to remove." The Decision agent stays out because a disagreement with the Gate would cost trust, and SM-4 is held back with it. Counter-metrics exist and are aimed: SM-C2 watches rubber-stamp acceptance, SM-C3 refuses a briefing-sized context dump. SM-2 matches the charging unit. That is a thesis with a scope, not a backlog with headings.

The numbers that can pass or kill the pilot do not test the two claims the vision bets on. A pilot can hit the adherence target from Boilerplate pins plus a strict CI Gate, with no evidence that Locked rules or the Guidance-to-Gate history are why anyone paid.

### Findings

- **medium** Primary metrics do not test the bet (§1, §6, §14 SM-1, SM-3) — The bet is "a Project tree whose Locked rules a child Project cannot drop, and a record that joins Guidance during construction with the later Gate result." The paid wall repeats that pair. SM-1 measures "mean Adherence of Applications created through the New-application flow, minus mean Adherence of the baseline set" and "Validates FR-9, FR-11, FR-14." It can be satisfied without a Locked rule ever binding, and without anyone reading the join. SM-3 asks only that Ghost applications trend "down," with no target, and a Ghost is an Application that reached the Gate with no Guidance, which is the absence of the join rather than its use. Consulted-but-deviated (FR-23) has no success metric. *Fix:* Add one pilot measure for the tree (for example, count of effective sets in which a Locked rule overrode a descendant) and one for the join (consulted-but-deviated rate, or a Buyer action taken from that list). Keep SM-1 as the adherence outcome and say which claim each number is allowed to settle.

- **medium** The bypass counter-metric cannot see the bypass it names (§14 SM-C1, §11, UJ-1) — SM-C1 is "Share of known building that reaches production with no Gate run," and it is what "means the Gate is pushing work outside." "Known" has no source. UJ-1's edge case is that "An Application published from a tool with no repository never appears," and the risk table says that inventory "undercounts real building." Work that skips the pipeline is exactly the work the product does not know about, so a rising SM-C1 cannot be the signal the mitigation depends on. *Fix:* Define the denominator (Company inventory, CI deploy events, or a declared census) and state that repository-less building stays outside SM-C1 until Open Question 2 has a channel.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs already carry a verifiable consequence: exit codes and an unmodified tree (FR-13, FR-14), a dropped Compilation with no partial payload (FR-26), cycle rejection and tenant isolation (FR-5), one acceptance record per selected Application (FR-18), a miss that does not invent a Standard (FR-12). The NFRs are bounds. "User-friendly," "gracefully," and "reasonable performance" do not appear. An engineer can story the shell of the registry, the CLI, and the boundary.

The center of the pilot does not close. Precedence, the code-stack Gate, the design-system Gate, and Accepted deviations are the behaviors a story must test, and each one uses an identity the PRD never defines. Downstream story creation will invent those keys.

### Findings

- **high** First-cut module entries do not carry the checks the Gate must emit (FR-2, FR-3, FR-14) — FR-2 limits a library entry to "a name, a version constraint, and a status of approved or prohibited," then requires a Deviation for "each missing required match." There is no required status, and languages and frameworks are in the requirement statement with no entry shape. FR-3 stores "a name, a usage rule, and the tokens it depends on," while the first-cut Gate evaluates "a token name, a forbidden import, or a component allow-list." FR-14 requires "one Correction instruction naming the Standard and a compliant alternative," and neither module has a field for that text or for the alternative. *Fix:* Define the first-cut entry types, including a status that distinguishes allowed from required, the machine-checkable design-system rule types, and where the Correction instruction comes from (authored on the Standard, or generated from those fields).

- **high** An Accepted deviation has no identity across revisions (FR-15, glossary "Deviation") — A Deviation is "a Gate finding that the Application does not follow a Standard." FR-15 says "While it is unexpired, the Gate treats that Deviation as accepted" and "After the end, the same Deviation fails the Gate again." A later run produces a new finding. Nothing says whether "same" means the same Standard id, the same offending dependency or component, or a fingerprint of the finding. Without that, the acceptance path that "ship[s] with the CI Gate in the first cut" cannot be tested, and FR-21's "open Deviation" and FR-23's "later Gate Deviation on S" will be implemented differently. *Fix:* Define the match key in the glossary and in FR-15. State that a new revision hits an unexpired acceptance only when that key matches, and that any other finding on the same Standard still fails.

- **high** Precedence never defines a conflict (FR-6, §5.2) — "On a conflict, the Standard scoped to the Project closest to the Application wins," and a Locked rule "is not replaced by a descendant." The only worked cases are "Parent allows library X and child prohibits library X" and the Locked inverse. The first cut also ships design-system Standards (tokens, components, usage rules). Two Standards can govern the same button, the same token, or overlapping libraries without sharing a name, and the resolver has no key that tells them apart. Locked rules are the bet; an invented key becomes the product. *Fix:* Define the conflict slot (for example library name, component name, token name) and state that Standards in different slots do not compete. Apply the same slot to the Locked-rule test.

- **medium** Application identity is "the same file" (FR-10, glossary "Project identification file") — "A Guidance event and a Gate run for the same file refer to the same Application id," and the file is what the New-application flow generates into a repository. The PRD does not say whether identity is a generated id inside the file, the file contents, or the repository path. A copied Boilerplate, or a second repository that starts from the same starter, either collides two Applications or splits the join FR-20 stores. *Fix:* State that the file carries an Application id minted at creation, that copying the file does not create a second history, and that Guidance refuses a duplicate id in another repository.

- **medium** The Guidance tool surface is a quality claim without a catalog (FR-11, FR-3, §5.5) — "The quality of the tool descriptions is part of the product," and each tool "states the decision it answers and the inputs it requires." The only call shape is FR-3's "a tool call that names the component or the usage situation." There is no first-cut list of decisions for the code-stack Module, for a miss, or for "which Standard applies." Story authors will each invent the MCP catalog the pilot has to prove. *Fix:* List the first-cut tools, the decision each one answers, and the inputs each one requires. Leave further tools as a non-goal.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are written down, not left for the reader to guess. §12 is a real non-goal list (no pull requests, no vendor hooks, no security certification, no seat charge, no OWASP scan). §13.2 repeats the deferred product and adds the note that change-acceptance is "emotionally load-bearing and easy to pull forward." Per-FR outs mark the Decision agent, tracker connectors, and lineage. The Assumptions Index is empty because those inferences were confirmed; that is an honest index, not a missing one. Nine open questions on a launch PRD would be a blocker if they were hidden. Most are correctly parked (name, liability, service levels, the two remaining board items).

The honesty fails where the in-scope list is quieter than the warnings. Two first-cut commitments are conditional in a note or an open question and unconditional in §13.1. One pilot prerequisite, how a person becomes a user, is in neither place.

### Findings

- **high** The registry is in the first cut and blocked on a decision the scope list does not carry (§13.1, FR-12 note, §11, Open Question 10) — §13.1 includes Standard publication, both first-cut Modules, and Document response (FR-1, FR-2, FR-3, FR-11). The note on FR-12 says "Before building the registry and the Document response, decide whether to adopt or contribute" to Packmind, and the risk table's mitigation is "adopt or contribute before building the registry." Open Question 10 points at that note. A breakdown taken from §13.1 will schedule the build the note forbids, or a team will treat the question as optional polish. *Fix:* Mark those four FRs in §13.1 as blocked on Open Question 10, and state the pilot clock starts after that decision, or after an explicit choice to build.

- **medium** Pilot access is neither specified nor excluded (UJ-1, UJ-2, §12, §13.2) — Journeys start "Authenticated on the management panel" and "Authenticated." SSO/SCIM is out of the first cut. No FR, non-goal, or open question says how a Company, a Buyer, a Builder, or a Standard owner gets an account, or whether the pilot uses invites, a customer IdP, or manually provisioned users. FR-4, FR-7, FR-8, FR-15, and FR-28 each assume a grant exists. *Fix:* Add a first-cut FR for provisioning those four grants, or add an explicit non-goal that the pilot's accounts are provisioned outside the product and name who holds that list.

- **medium** Panel stories are in scope while their payload is an open question (§13.1, FR-26, FR-28, Open Question 5) — §13.1 requires Collectors, Compilations, and the Adherence, gap, inventory, Ghost, and consulted-but-deviated views. Open Question 5 says the field list "Blocks the pilot security review." The note on FR-28 says it "does not block the shape of FR-26," which is true of the drop-on-schema-failure rule and silent about FR-21 through FR-23. Those views are not acceptable as done until the fields exist. *Fix:* Either publish the minimum Compilation fields the panel FRs already imply (identifiers and aggregates, no prompt text) as the first-cut schema, or mark FR-21, FR-22, and FR-23 in §13.1 as blocked on Open Question 5.

## Downstream usability — strong

This PRD is chain-top: the document purpose names UX, architecture, and the epic breakdown as readers. They can source-extract. The glossary is binding ("Use these terms exactly"), FR-1 through FR-28, UJ-1 through UJ-5, SM-1 through SM-5, SM-C1 through SM-C3, and NFR-1 through NFR-5 are contiguous and unique, and the journey and metric cross-references resolve. Each UJ has a named protagonist and inline context. Sections cite glossary terms and FR ids rather than "see above."

One scope word is not in that glossary, and the document uses it for two different horizons. Story extraction will mis-file anything labeled only "v1."

### Findings

- **medium** "v1," "first cut," and "MVP" are different horizons written as one (§9, §12, §13) — §13 is titled "MVP Scope" and then specifies "The first cut." §12 uses both "v1 does not charge per seat" / "v1 does not scan" and "The first cut does not include the Decision agent…" §9's "In v1" list is the integration set, not the pilot set. A story pulled from "v1" can be read as pilot-now or as the whole first product generation (no write API, no OWASP, no seats). *Fix:* Define the three terms once. Use "first cut" or "MVP" for the pilot list in §13, and use "v1" only for constraints that outlast the pilot.

## Shape fit — strong

Launch-grade multi-stakeholder B2B, chain-top, with a real panel and a real agent workflow. Five journeys with named protagonists are the right weight: Vini's inventory, Ana's placement, Ana's Guidance call, Ana's Gate, Helena's later change. The capability sections (registry, tree, Gate, Compilation boundary) belong beside those journeys, not instead of them. There is no standalone persona chapter and no journey pasted on for a single operator. Data governance, monetization, and integration are present because the product carries those concerns. UJ-5 is fully specified and marked outside the first cut, so later scope is not disguised as pilot scope.

The Buyer's control decision, which is the paid tier, is still only a branch inside the Builder's Gate journey. UX can draft it from FR-15, and will be guessing the session.

### Findings

- **low** The Buyer never has a session for the acceptance the product exists to make cheap (UJ-4, FR-15, §1) — The vision says the Buyer "must hold Standards without becoming the bottleneck." UJ-4's protagonist is Ana: she "asks the Buyer to accept the Deviation with a justification," and the climax is that an Accepted deviation "is on record." Vini's only journey is the inventory (UJ-1). The justification, the period, and the alert interval have fields and no session. *Fix:* Add a short UJ, with Vini as protagonist, for accepting one Deviation and later receiving the recurring alert. Point it at FR-15.
