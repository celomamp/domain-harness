# Reconciliation: product definition → PRD

Input: `docs/harness-de-dominio.md` (Harness de Domínio — definição de produto, documento de trabalho)
PRD: `prd.md`
Addendum: `addendum.md`
Compared: 2026-10-09. The PRD was not rewritten.

## What the PRD captured

The four jobs are in the vision and the feature set: record Standards in Modules, deliver the one that applies at decision time over MCP, check before production with the Gate, and join consultation with the Gate result. The three roles (Buyer, Builder, Standard owner), the six module kinds, and the first-cut pair (code stack, design system) with information sources next are in the glossary and §13. The Project tree, precedence, Locked rules, and permission to create a child are FR-5–FR-7. The new-application flow, Boilerplate, and the hidden project file as the join key are FR-8–FR-10. Document versus Decision response, the rejection of vendor hooks, session briefings, end-of-turn review, and automatic pull requests are in FR-11–FR-12, §10, the non-goals, and the addendum’s rejected-mechanisms table. Accepted deviations with a Buyer-set alert interval, impact, batch acceptance, and tracker tasks are FR-15–FR-19. The hybrid boundary, deterministic auditable Compilations, and zero retention that also binds the model provider are FR-26–FR-28 and the addendum. Open items from §13 of the source (name, Lovable, decision-agent quality operations, contractual limits, compilation fields, privacy) are Open Questions 1–6. Ticket and Corteva are named in the addendum. What Levva keeps if the product stops is in the addendum’s last paragraph.

## Gaps

Ideas in the source that the PRD or the addendum dropped. Qualitative lines the FR structure flattens are listed first.

1. **Lock versus release, and the missing mandate.** Source §2 and §8 (point 3). Companies today either review everything by hand and become the bottleneck (`Travar`) or let each person configure their own agent so patterns stay scattered and partly undocumented (`Liberar`). Both are called bad. The bottleneck is not technical skill: a technology team could assemble a homemade version and does not, because operations absorb them and nobody has the mandate to centralize patterns for agents. The platform and the service take that mandate. The Ticket evidence is sharper than UJ-1: connecting Claude Code to Figma was easy; what Vini still lacked was centralization, standardization, and validation. The PRD’s Why Now is a market window. “Without becoming the bottleneck” survives in the Buyer job, and the homemade-repo contrast is only partly in the vision. The paired failure modes and the mandate sentence are gone, so the product reads as a tool category instead of the answer to “nobody inside the customer owns this.” **Where:** PRD vision (problem framing) and addendum (why the service sits beside the software).

2. **Two ways to sell the same product, and architecture as a threatened sponsor.** Source §4. Control, the Ticket case: know what is being built and that it follows the patterns. Acceleration, the Corteva hypothesis: architecture teams that block work for lack of trust could release more if the check were automatic; they stop being the area people route around and become module owners and sponsors; the sale is told in time-to-approval. The risk sits in the same paragraph: architecture can read the platform as a threat to its role. The addendum names Corteva once, as an unsigned acceleration hypothesis. PRD Open Question 7 retires time-to-approval from the first cut. The sponsorship reframe and the threat are not in the risk table. **Where:** addendum commercial narrative, and PRD §11 for the threat. Not omitted: the source holds both motions as present ways to sell, not as a later story.

3. **“Começar certo vale mais que corrigir depois.”** Source §11, from the effect stated in §5.3. Starting right is worth more than correcting later. The new-application flow and the Boilerplate are the main adherence mechanism: the application is born adherent, and it exists for IT from the first moment, not only when it reaches the Gate. UJ-2, FR-8, and FR-9 build the flow, and SM-1 measures it, but nothing states the decision rule. The vision opens on system of record and proof of adherence, which makes the Gate feel like the main mechanism and the Boilerplate a convenience. **Where:** PRD, as a standing principle next to the neutrality and no-edit guardrails in §10.

4. **Description quality is a core competence, and the reports have a diagnosis.** Source §5.5 and §6. The result also depends on the tool, the agent, and the person operating it, so the quality of the tool descriptions is a central competence of the product, not a field on the tool schema. Ignored modules usually mean a bad description, not a bad pattern. “Consulted but deviated” has three readings: an ambiguous pattern, a bad answer, or a real need the pattern does not cover. A recurring deviation is the signal that the pattern is obsolete, and governance is supposed to learn from use. FR-11 requires a description. FR-22 and FR-23 count ignored modules, misses, and consulted-but-deviated. The meaning of those counts is gone, so a panel can ship as a list. **Where:** PRD notes on §5.5, FR-22, and FR-23.

5. **A stale harness is worse than none.** Source §12. An out-of-date harness is worse than no harness, because the agent then imposes an abandoned pattern with confidence. The risk row in PRD §11 keeps the mechanism (imposed with confidence) and points at ignored modules and an owner. It drops the comparison that should bias scope: a module you will not maintain should not be served. That bias is what makes first-cut discipline a product principle rather than a scheduling choice. The same section says each way of keeping patterns current, and each module, is almost a product of its own. **Where:** PRD §11 and §13, as the reason a thin first cut is preferable to a broad registry.

6. **Deviations have severity.** Source §6, validation side. The report is the most frequent deviations, by severity. FR-21 has frequency by Application, Project, and Module, and no severity on a Deviation. Nothing else in the glossary or the Gate FRs introduces it. **Where:** PRD glossary and FR-21 (and the Gate result, if severity is assigned when the finding is emitted).

7. **The software is meant to feed the services pipeline.** Source §6 (closing) and §9. Gaps, obsolete patterns, and impact analyses create natural demand for maintenance. Keeping patterns current is a combination the playbook defines: the platform as the official source, integration with origin systems, one owner per module with a review process, and the maintenance contract. Implementation is not only installing the Gate: explore the client’s areas, find the patterns that exist, and when they do not exist, propose how to create them. Maintenance is contracted by person-hour or person-month. The PRD says services are not features and that the registry must be updatable without a product change. The addendum prices playbook, diagnostic, implementation, and a monthly maintenance band. The causal line (these reports exist so the service has a pipeline), the “propose them when they are missing” shape of implementation, and the person-hour contract are not there. **Where:** addendum. A one-line note on FR-22 is enough on the PRD side so the reports stay named in a way a service engagement can pick up.

8. **What the first cut is for in the room.** Source §10. Start narrow on both sides: MCP and Gate, end to end, few modules. The Gate proves the value in numbers; the MCP is what has commercial appeal in the demonstration. Code stack is first because it is the easiest to check automatically and it speaks directly to IT’s pain. Design system is first because it demos best and it is where Ticket’s real request started. The PRD keeps the module order and the “risk sits here” reason for information sources. It does not keep the demonstration pairing or the reason for each of the two modules. §6 then treats document-response MCP as the free funnel and puts the paid appeal on the tree, Locked rules, Accepted deviations, and the join. **Where:** PRD §13, with the commercial pairing in the addendum’s pilot section.

### Correctly omitted

- **Naming people in the builder inventory.** Source §6 lists areas, people, and tools. The same section, §12, and §13 say that recording what business people ask agents is sensitive and must be decided early. FR-20 keeping individual Builder names off until the privacy rules exist follows that caution. **Where:** correctly omitted for now; Open Question 6 is the place it returns.
- **Board figures for investment, headcount, and deadline.** Source §14 says those are still to be defined and gives examples of a pilot bar (a paying customer, a measured adherence on a real customer, or a letter of intent from Ticket). It does not assert numbers. The addendum’s R$ 1 million / six months / team of four comes from the executive analysis, not from a dropped paragraph of this source. **Where:** correctly absent from this source. The loss of the Ticket letter as a sufficient example is a conflict below, not a missing requirement.

## Conflicts

Places the PRD contradicts the source.

1. **Who answers for the standard and the validation.** Source §8 (point 2) and §12. The reason to buy a platform rather than a folder is that a company answers for the pattern and for the validation, instead of a repository maintained by whoever has time. The buyer’s line in the risks is “a company I can blame,” which the contract then has to bound if an application that passed the Gate later has a security failure. PRD §1 says “A Company is accountable for the Standard and for the Gate,” and the glossary defines Company as the customer. Later in the same section the product is accountable for the Effective Standard set and the join. §10 says a passing Gate is not a certification and moves liability to a legal workstream. Customer ownership of the content is compatible with the source. Assigning accountability for the Gate to the customer, and reducing the vendor to the join, contradicts §8’s responsibility claim and drops the promise §12 says must be negotiated rather than denied.

2. **What the free core is.** Source §9. The free or open-source nucleus is registration of patterns and MCP with a document response, and it works as a funnel. The paid plan is the decision agent, intelligence, and reports. PRD §6 Community adds the CLI Gate and a one-Project limit. The two bullets that “both sources already agree” on (document response free; decision response and the broader reports paid) do match §9. The CLI Gate inside the free nucleus does not: §9 defines that nucleus by listing what it contains, and the Gate is not in the list. §5.6 presents the Gate as a CI step, not as a free local command.

3. **The primary sales metric is still open, and the two motions are simultaneous.** Source §13 leaves the main sales metric undecided between percent of adherent applications and time to approval (the Corteva case). §4 presents those as two ways to sell the same product now. PRD Open Question 7 marks the question resolved: the first sale is adherence and the join; time-to-approval is a later story and not a first-cut metric. That closes a decision the source left open and turns a parallel motion into a sequence.

4. **The first cut is wider than “narrow on both sides,” and the pilot bar is harder than the source’s examples.** Source §10 limits the first cut to MCP and the Gate, end to end, with few modules. The tree, the identification file, and the Boilerplate are implied by §5 and by the principle in §11, so their presence in §13.1 is not a contradiction. The read API (§5.1 in the source, full product) and the intelligence catalog in §6 (demand, gaps, ignored modules, inventory, ghosts, consulted-but-deviated, proof of effect) are not in the first cut the source describes. PRD §13.1 requires the read API, Collectors, and those panel views in the first cut. Separately, source §14’s illustrative success bar is a paying customer, or a measured adherence, or a letter of intent from Ticket. PRD SM-5 requires a paid pilot of at least R$ 150 thousand or an annual contract, and at least three letters of intent or paid diagnostics. A Ticket letter alone no longer qualifies.
